theia Rust · P2P overlay + services

A public-key-addressed P2P overlay and service layer I architected and built, for SSH, file transfer, port forwarding and fetching web content through another device. You reach a peer by its ed25519 key instead of its address, and every service behind that reach is gated by a capability rooted in the same key, verified offline without a central authorization service. Access is given per service, can expire on a date, and is taken back at the host: a revoke ends an open session, ssh included, in under a second, and leaves everyone else connected. The networking underneath is pluggable: iroh today (its hole-punching does the NAT traversal) and quirk, a UDP stack I wrote from scratch to understand how a modern transport works; by design the reach layer extends to a purpose-built transport per medium (Bluetooth and other non-IP radios), which is a goal, not yet built. A shared conformance suite tests every transport against the same identity contract, rejecting implementations that misattribute the peer they reached. An independent reference transport keeps the interface from overfitting to iroh. It's a stack of small Rust crates:

  • bifrost (reach): opens a byte-stream to a machine named only by its ed25519 key, carried over a swappable transport. Finding the peer across NATs is the transport's job (iroh's hole-punching); you address who a peer is, not where.
  • nauthy (the gate): one ed25519 key mints, narrows, delegates, and revokes capability tokens, each verified offline with no coordinator, rooted in the key a transport handshake already proved. Attenuable, expiring, and revocable through a denylist the node checks at admission.
  • tightbeam (the tunnel): publishes your local services by name behind the gate, runs a short handshake that selects one and confirms admission before a byte flows, then hands back a plain two-way stream, so any program speaking a TCP port or Unix socket rides it unchanged.
  • services (the engines): standalone units, fetch, measure, sshh (keyless SSH), and transfer, that each do one job on an already-admitted stream, embeddable one at a time.
  • swoosh (the product): one CLI that binds the layers into human verbs, ssh in, send files, forward ports, fetch through a peer, hand out revocable access, all by public key, with no IP addresses to manage.

Approach

I look for problems just beyond what I know how to solve. Even a failed experiment can expose a constraint and lead to a better design. Performance matters, but so do elegance and intuitiveness: the machinery should be fast without making the person using it carry all its complexity. With theia, I want nearby machines to keep working together over a local link rather than depend on public infrastructure. Identity and access stay under the hosts' control, and I want the system to remain useful when you have to run every piece yourself.
