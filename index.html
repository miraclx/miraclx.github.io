Selected work

theia Rust · P2P overlay + services

A public-key-addressed, control-plane-free peer-to-peer overlay I architected and built solo. Reach any machine by its ed25519 identity, across NATs, with no address, account, or server in between. The transport is a trait, not a network: iroh is one implementation, quirk (a QUIC-style UDP stack I wrote from scratch) is another, and the design reaches a key over any medium that can carry a stream, from UDP today to non-IP radios like BLE, Wi-Fi HaLow, and Thread. A conformance suite ships decoy transports to keep every implementation honest, so none passes by accident. It's a stack of small Rust crates:

  • bifrost (reach): open a byte-stream to a machine by its public key, across NATs, over any transport. You address who a peer is, not where.
  • nauthy (the gate): capability tokens rooted at one ed25519 key you hold, verified offline with no server and no PKI; attenuable, expiring, revocable.
  • tightbeam (the tunnel): a gated byte-stream to a named service on a peer, addressed by key, composing bifrost's reach with nauthy's gate. A directed beam to one peer, never a broadcast.
  • services (the engines): fetch, measure, sshh (keyless SSH), and transfer, each doing one job on an already-admitted stream.
  • swoosh (the product): one CLI where reach, the gate, and the services become one tool. ssh in, send files, forward ports, fetch through a peer, share access, all by public key, no IPs, no servers.

Approach

I build systems where control sits with the person using them, not with a company in the middle. In the theia-hq overlay, your identity is a key you hold, not a row in someone's database, so there is no account to register, no dashboard to suspend, and nothing anyone can deplatform. I reach for the smallest design that does the job, which sometimes means writing a layer from scratch to understand it and then deliberately not shipping it. The test I hold work to is whether it still answers to you when the vendor, the server, and the network in the middle are all gone.
