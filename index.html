Selected work

theia Rust · P2P overlay + services

A public-key-addressed, control-plane-free peer-to-peer overlay I architected and built solo. Reach any machine by its ed25519 identity, across NATs, with no address, account, or server in between. I generalized the transport behind a single trait: it runs on iroh or on a QUIC-style UDP transport I wrote from scratch, proven substitutable under one conformance suite that ships decoy transports, so it can't pass by accident. It's built as a stack of small Rust crates, each earning its own boundary:

  • bifrost (reach): open a byte-stream to a machine by its public key, across NATs, over any transport. You address who a peer is, not where.
  • nauthy (the gate): capability tokens rooted at one ed25519 key you hold, verified offline with no server and no PKI; attenuable, expiring, revocable.
  • tightbeam (the tunnel): a gated byte-stream to a named service on a peer, addressed by key, composing bifrost's reach with nauthy's gate. A directed beam to one peer, never a broadcast.
  • services (the engines): fetch, measure, sshh (keyless SSH), and transfer, each doing one job on an already-admitted stream.
  • swoosh (the product): one CLI where reach, the gate, and the services become one tool. ssh in, send files, forward ports, fetch through a peer, share access, all by public key, no IPs, no servers.

Approach

I build systems where control sits with the person using them, not with a company in the middle. My work in the theia-hq overlay starts from a simple inversion: your identity should be a key you hold, not a row in someone's database, so there is no account to register, no dashboard to suspend, and nothing anyone can deplatform. I reach for the smallest design that makes the illegal states unrepresentable, which sometimes means writing a layer from scratch to understand it and then deliberately not shipping it as a product. I care about naming things plainly, letting each piece earn its own boundary, and writing so a stranger understands what a thing is before they know its history. The test I hold work to is whether it still answers to you when the vendor, the server, and the network in the middle are all gone.
